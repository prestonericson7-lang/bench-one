Wiring the Stack

Four processors on one deck, and the exact pin-to-pin map that ties them together — without touching the Teensy↔ESP link you already built. The Luckfox conducts. Two Teensys do the work. The ESP is still the radio. Every wire below is here so you can build from it at the bench and miss nothing.

01

Who does what, and who talks to whom

“Brain and Radio” settled one rule: mechanism lives on the radio, policy lives on the brain. Adding two nodes doesn’t break that rule — it stacks it. Intent flows down; raw facts flow up. Each node only ever knows enough to do its own job.

The rule, extended

The Luckfox decides what. Teensy 1 decides when and how. Teensy 2 does the math. The ESP moves the air. Nothing above reaches down past the node below it — the Luckfox never touches the 802.11 MAC, it asks Teensy 1, which asks the radio. That is what keeps each firmware small and each node replaceable.

The topology

Three UART links, each one point-to-point, plus one shared bus hanging off the master. The pin pairs on every arrow are exactly what you land on the breadboard.

03

The three UART links, pin for pin

All three run at 921600 8N1 and all three are 3.3 V on both ends, so every one is a direct wire — no level shifter in any inter-processor link. TX always crosses to RX. Ground is shared across the whole stack, not per-link.

Why the worker hangs off the master, not the Luckfox

It keeps the worker’s job stream on a hard-real-time 600 MHz peer instead of behind Linux scheduling jitter, and it leaves the master as the one place that knows the whole system’s state. Worker pins p2–p41 stay completely free for whatever the job needs. If you’d rather the Luckfox drive the worker directly, that’s one of the switches in §08.

The one 138 gotcha

Exactly one output is low at all times while the decoder is enabled — there is no “none selected” address. That is what pin 33 is for: hold the gate high, set the address, then drop the gate to assert one CS, and raise it again before changing the address. It turns a decoder into a clean chip-select mux.

The 74HC595 (serial→parallel out) and 74HC165 (parallel→serial in) ride the same SPI0: SCK=13, MOSI=11 into the 595 chain, MISO=12 out of the 165 chain, with the 595 latch and 165 load taken from two of the 138’s Y lines. The 74HC4051 gives you 8 analog inputs through one ADC pin, and the 74HC373 latches a parallel bus. Which of these get populated depends on what you’re driving — see §08.

05

Level shifting — where your 3 TXB and 3 TXS go

Here is the short version: at 3.3 V across the whole stack, you need zero shifters between processors. All three MCUs are 3.3 V logic, and 74HC parts run happily at 3.3 V too. The six shifters are for the day a 5 V part joins the bus — and then which one you grab matters.

The Teensy is not 5 V tolerant

An i.MX RT1062 pin takes 3.3 V, full stop. Put 5 V on a Teensy GPIO and you damage it. So any 5 V sensor, 5 V display, or 5 V-only logic chip goes through a shifter before it ever reaches a Teensy pin — this is the whole reason the parts are in the kit.

PartYou haveUse it forNever use it for
TXS01083 I2C and any open-drain bus crossing voltages — a 5 V I2C sensor, or a 5 V MCP bank. It has the weak pull-ups and auto-direction that I2C needs. High-speed push-pull SPI — its pull-ups round off fast edges.
TXB01083 Push-pull signals crossing voltages — SPI, UART, clocks, a 5 V shift-register chain. Faster, cleaner edges. I2C. It fights the bus pull-ups and the direction never settles.

Rule of thumb: two wires that pull each other up (I2C) → TXS. Everything with a real driver on each end (SPI, UART) → TXB. Both want VCCA on the low side, VCCB on the high side, and /OE tied high through 10k.

06

Power and ground

One 5 V rail from the battery bank feeds every board’s own regulator; the fabric runs on 3.3 V. Everything shares one ground — a floating ground is what your “Brain and Radio” doc caught as a whole class of link failure.

Cut the VUSB–VIN pad on both Teensys

You want USB plugged in to flash while the stack is also on external 5 V. If you don’t cut the VUSB–VIN jumper on the back of each Teensy 4.1, those two 5 V sources fight and you back-power your laptop’s USB through the rail. Cut it, and each Teensy takes 5 V from VIN while USB still handles data. Standard Teensy step; easy to forget with two of them.

Feed the radio or it browns out

The ESP32 pulls ~500 mA spikes on WiFi TX — that is the BROWNOUT reset reason your radio already reports. Give the 5 V rail real current, put a 10–100 µF bulk cap right at the ESP, and a 100 nF across every IC’s VCC–GND next to the pin. You have the caps; use them generously here.

07

One protocol, four nodes

The cleanest part of the whole design costs nothing: every link is point-to-point, so interop_protocol.h — same frame, same 0xAA start, same CRC-8, same SEQ 0x00 for unsolicited — is reused byte-for-byte on all three. No addresses, no rewrite. All that’s new is which command numbers live on which wire.

RangeMeaningLives onStatus
0x01–0x0FSystem — PING/PONG, READY, NACKevery linkshared, as-is
0x10–0x1FWiFiLink B (radio)existing
0x20–0x2FBLELink B (radio)your milestone 4
0x30–0x3FWorker jobs — submit, result, statusLink C (worker)new
0x40–0x4FOrchestrator — task, telemetry, logLink A (Luckfox)new
0x50–0x5FFabric / IO — expander + display opson-chipnew

This keeps your load-bearing walls intact: WiFi still owns 0x1x, BLE still owns the reserved 0x2x. The new ranges slot above them without renumbering a single existing command.

08

Calls I made — flip any of them

You told me to design the connections, not to invent the mission. So here is every decision I made that you might want made differently, in one place. Say the word on any row and I’ll redraw the affected wires and the code before you cut anything.

DecisionWhat I choseWhyFlip it if…
Topology Luckfox → Teensy1 → {ESP32, Teensy2} Keeps Link B byte-for-byte; one clean chain; every hop point-to-point so the protocol needs no addressing. you want the Luckfox to own the ESP directly, or the worker to hang off the Luckfox.
Fabric voltage 3.3 V everywhere Teensy isn’t 5 V tolerant; all three MCUs are 3.3 V, so nothing needs shifting and nothing can fry a Teensy. you specifically want a 5 V fabric — then the TXB/TXS go inline where I show them.
